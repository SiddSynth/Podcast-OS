Host: Welcome back to another episode. Today we have Nikhil Kamath, co-founder of Zerodha. Nikhil, welcome.
Nikhil Kamath: Thanks for having me, glad to be here.
Host: Let's talk about startups. Most founders fail. Why do you think bootstrapping Zerodha worked when so many funded brokerages died?
Nikhil Kamath: Startups fail because founders focus on building product in a vacuum without talking to clients. You need to validate product-market fit on day one. When we co-founded Zerodha, we spent the first years just interacting with customers, solving their support issues, and building trust. That's why we bootstrapped successfully. Profits give you leverage, whereas venture capital forces you onto a hamster wheel of vanity metrics.
Host: What's your view on buying a house in India?
Nikhil Kamath: I became a billionaire and the first thing I did was sign another rental lease. Real estate is leverage-heavy and illiquid. Renting gives you flexibility to deploy capital in high-growth equities or startups.
